## 1. Should we bring the Chicco high-chair attachment?

### Why this needs another look

The current website recommends leaving the Chicco attachment at home. Luna reasonably doubts that restaurants in mainland China—especially hole-in-the-wall restaurants and places encountered while traveling—will reliably provide safe, usable high chairs. The family also expects a significant amount of home dining. Requiring one adult to lap-feed Zuzu repeatedly would be tiring and would make shared meals less enjoyable.

### Questions to assess

- How reliably can we expect high chairs at the kinds of restaurants and homes we will visit in Taiwan, Nanjing, and Yunnan?
- How often will we eat in private homes, rentals, or other places without toddler seating?
- What are the exact packed dimensions and weight of our particular Chicco chair attachment?
- How much setup time does it require, and what table shapes, thicknesses, or edge designs are incompatible with it?
- Is it safe and practical for the tables commonly found in the places we expect to visit?
- Does its daily usefulness justify its luggage volume across a four-week trip?
- Are there meaningfully smaller or lighter travel alternatives that remain stable, washable, and compatible with a broad range of chairs or tables?
- Would a fabric chair harness, compact booster, or other alternative solve the problem, and what safety or compatibility tradeoffs would that introduce?
- If we leave it home, what is the realistic fallback for home meals and restaurants without high chairs?

### Decision needed

Choose one of these outcomes after research and a packed-size comparison:

1. Bring the existing Chicco attachment.
2. Buy and bring a smaller travel alternative.
3. Leave toddler seating home and accept a clearly defined fallback plan.
